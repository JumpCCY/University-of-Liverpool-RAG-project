Question: if a student is good at maths, are there modules that suit them?

- **COMP116 Analytic Techniques for Computer Science** — year 1, compulsory. Covers Linear Algebra (Matrix Theory), statistics, introductory calculus (limits, continuity, differentiation, integration), complex numbers, and potentially Information Theory.

- **COMP331 Optimisation** — year 3, optional. An in-depth tour of optimisation methods applied to various models.
- **Algorithms and Optimisation pathway** — a named specialist pathway available for graduation, built on these types of modules.

We don't hold information linking specific module choices directly to A-level maths grades, but the core year 1 modules above provide the rigorous mathematical grounding for all students.

Question: do we have modules on AI?
Answer: **Yes** — AI runs right through the degree.

- **COMP111 Introduction to Artificial Intelligence** — year 1, compulsory. Covers search, reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, learning, plus history, philosophy, and ethics of AI.
- **COMP219 Advanced Artificial Intelligence** — year 2, optional. Focuses on machine learning, deep learning, probabilistic graphical models, and the safety/security of ML in real-world deployment.
- **COMP304 Knowledge Representation and Reasoning** — year 3, optional. Formal ways to reason about knowledge and uncertain information.
- **COMP310 Multi-Agent Systems** — year 3, optional. How multiple software agents cooperate to solve complex problems.
- **COMP329 Autonomous Mobile Robotics** — year 3, optional. Principles of robotics and autonomous systems.
- **ELEC320 Neural Networks** — year 3, optional. Introduction to neural network theory and applications in AI.

It starts compulsory in year 1 with core concepts and ethics, then deepens through optional modules in years 2 and 3 covering machine learning, reasoning, robotics, and multi-agent systems. Students can also graduate on the dedicated **Artificial Intelligence pathway**.

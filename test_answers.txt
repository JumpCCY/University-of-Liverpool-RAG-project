Question: what are the short 7-credit modules?
Answer: There are seven 7-credit modules available, all optional. They are split between Year 2 and Year 3.

**Year 2**
- **COMP221 Planning Your Career** — Semester 1. Focuses on employability skills for placements or graduate jobs.
- **COMP281 Principles of C and Memory Management** — Semester 2. Covers low-level programming in C for computationally intensive tasks.
- **COMP282 The C++ Programming Language** — Semester 2. Extends C with object-oriented principles and graphical application development.
- **COMP284 Scripting Languages** — Semester 2. Covers JavaScript and PHP for web and computer-based applications.
- **COMP285 Computer Aided Software Development** — Semester 2. Theory and practice of tools in the software development lifecycle.

**Year 3**
- **ELEC319 Image Processing** — Semester 1. Fundamentals of image generation, representation, compression, and feature extraction.
- **ELEC320 Neural Networks** — Semester 2. Introduction to neural network theory, applications, and AI.

Question: if a student is good at maths, are there modules that suit them?
Answer: **Yes** — there are several modules that heavily utilise mathematical foundations, particularly in years 1 and 2.

- **COMP109 Foundations of Computer Science** — year 1, compulsory. Provides the mathematical foundation, tools, and proof techniques necessary for the degree.
- **COMP116 Analytic Techniques for Computer Science** — year 1, compulsory. Covers Linear Algebra (Matrix Theory), statistics, introductory calculus (limits, continuity, differentiation, integration), complex numbers, and information theory.
- **COMP229 Introduction to Data Science** — year 2, optional. Starts from fundamental mathematical methods including Linear Algebra, Metric Geometry, Graph Theory, and Computational Geometry.
- **COMP331 Optimisation** — year 3, optional. An in-depth tour of optimisation methods applied to various models.

Students can also specialise further by choosing the **Algorithms and Optimisation** or **Data Science** pathways in years 2 and 3, which build on these analytical skills.

Question: do we have modules on AI?
Answer: **Yes** — AI runs right through the degree.

- **COMP111 Introduction to Artificial Intelligence** — year 1, compulsory. Covers search, reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, learning, plus history, philosophy, and ethics of AI.
- **COMP219 Advanced Artificial Intelligence** — year 2, optional. Focuses on machine learning, deep learning, probabilistic graphical models, and the safety/security of ML in real-world deployment.
- **COMP304 Knowledge Representation and Reasoning** — year 3, optional. Formal ways to reason about knowledge and uncertain information.
- **COMP338 Computer Vision** — year 3, optional. Closely aligned with AI; covers traditional techniques and deep learning-based methods for 2D/3D vision.
- **COMP222 Principles of Computer Games Design and Implementation** — year 2, optional. Includes advanced AI techniques for game control procedures.
- **COMP342 Advanced Topics in Computer Game Development** — year 3, optional. Covers game AI, content generation, graphics, physics, and sound.
- **COMP218 Introduction to Theory of Computation** — year 2, optional. Illustrates the importance of automata and computation models in Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence.

It starts compulsory in year 1 with core concepts and ethics, then deepens through optional modules in years 2 and 3 covering machine learning, computer vision, and game AI.
